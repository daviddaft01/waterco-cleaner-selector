Hydraulics: why suction cleaners stall

A suction cleaner only works inside a suction window. Every restriction between the cleaner and the pump takes some of it away.
  • Too little suction: it stalls, sits in one spot or won’t climb walls. Too much: it races, lifts off the floor or draws air, which can starve the pump. Kreepy Krauly’s Smart-Skim and Auto Skim valves vent excess vacuum automatically; The PoolCleaner uses its regulator valve.
  • Operating suction on a 1 hp pump (about 45 kPa available): VTX-3 and VTX-7 about 18 kPa; Sprinta Plus and rX-Tank PRO about 25 kPa (Kreepy Krauly training). The PoolCleaner: at least 8 wheel RPM, about 18–25 cm of mercury on a vacuum gauge (manual; gauge not supplied).
  • Resistance adds up: a long suction line, a pump above pool level, a second skimmer or an open main drain all reduce the vacuum at the cleaner. Close every suction line except the cleaner’s.
  • Pipe run and pipe size: distance alone doesn’t stop a suction cleaner; pipe size and pump power overcome the friction loss. Waterco training guidance: under 10 m, a 1 hp pump with 40 or 50 mm pipe works well; 10–25 m needs 50 mm lines and a 1–1.5 hp pump; over 25 m, a suction cleaner becomes inefficient, so consider a robot.
  • Hose length: the corrugated cleaner hose loses far more suction than smooth PVC pipe. Fit only enough hose to reach the farthest point from the skimmer, plus the maker’s allowance. Extra lengths reduce suction.
  • Signs the run is too long or restricted: air pockets in the pump lid, or low suction at the skimmer with the pump at full speed and a clean filter.
  • Variable-speed pumps: the lowest speed may not drive the cleaner. Program the cleaning hours about 200 rpm higher (Kreepy Krauly training).
  • The clean filter paradox: it runs well after the filter is cleaned, then slows as debris loads the filter and pressure rises. Note the clean filter pressure at install. If the cleaner stops after a few days, backwash or clean the filter and empty the baskets before suspecting the cleaner.
Pipe-run distances and pipe sizes are Waterco training guidance from field experience, consistent with PVC friction-loss estimates. Model suction figures come from the makers’ manuals and training.
